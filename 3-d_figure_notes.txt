1. Top-Left Panel: ⓵ Filled Ball
What it shows: A solid 3D sphere rendered out of individual points (voxels) on a 3D coordinate grid [1].
The Interpretation: This is your source volume or target object. In a simulation, this represents the actual physical matter you are testing—such as a tumor, a kidney stone, or a solid metal part. It tells the computer exactly where the object's physical mass exists in 3D space.
2. Top-Right Panel: ⓶ Surface Shell
What it shows: The exact same sphere from the first panel, but completely hollowed out so only the outer skin remains [1].
The Interpretation: In wave physics and acoustics software, this represents the sensor mask or transducer array [1]. It defines the precise boundary where waves either start or stop. If you were modeling a medical ultrasound scan, this shell tells the computer exactly where to place the virtual microphone sensors to listen to incoming echoes.
3. Bottom-Left Panel: ⓷ XY Slice at depth=25
What it shows: A 2D cross-section cut right through the middle of your 3D space, showing a red-to-blue temperature/pressure heatmap with a yellow dotted boundary line [1].
The Interpretation: This is your material property or wave intensity map. The yellow line proves your code knows exactly where the boundary of the sphere is on a 2D plane. The smooth gradient background shows that the physical properties (like density, sound speed, or initial acoustic pressure) change smoothly across the space rather than cutting off instantly.
4. Bottom-Right Panel: ⓸ Octree Cells Inside vs Outside Sphere
What it shows: The entire 3D space broken down into an adaptive point cloud, color-coded with red dots inside the sphere shape and cyan dots outside [1].
The Interpretation: This is your computational efficiency map. It proves that your Octree algorithm is sorting the data correctly [1]. It shows that the computer successfully separates the environment into two distinct zones: the target zone (red) and the surrounding background environment (cyan) [1].
